# Comedy Slate — Business Owner Stories

**Finished length:** 30–45s · **Built from:** 4–8s shots, plus a 15s clip where a
performance has to hold · **Assembled in:** Premiere

> Supersedes `nike-style-slate.md`. That register was heroic and cold. This one
> is warm, funny, and a little cheesy on purpose.

## The rule set

- **Never say how it's made.** No tech words. No "AI," no "agents," no
  "automation," no "platform." The videos are about *them*, not about us.
- **The joke is never at their expense.** We laugh *with* the owner. They're
  competent, proud, and doing their best — the marketing is what's failing them,
  not their character. Punch at the bedsheet, never at the baker.
- **Specific beats clever.** "A muffin on a wrinkled bedsheet under a desk lamp"
  is funny. "Bad product photos" is not.
- **Cheesy is allowed.** Warmth reads as honest. Polish reads as an agency.

## The comedy engines we're using

| Engine | How it works |
|---|---|
| Deadpan contrast | Treat something trivial with total cinematic seriousness |
| Escalation | A small problem quietly becomes absurd |
| The slow reveal | Pull back to show what we were *really* looking at |
| Confident wrongness | A character is completely sure, completely wrong, totally lovable |

## 01 — THE NEPHEW
**~40s · engine: escalation · UGC-adjacent**

A proud owner stands in his shop. "My nephew does all our marketing. He's got
an eye."

Cut to the nephew. He is fourteen. He is filming vertically with his thumb
covering a third of the lens. He is eating a bag of chips *while* filming. He
says "yeah just like, do something" to a man who has run this business for
twenty-two years.

Cut to the finished edit playing on the shop's TV. There is a Minecraft
transition. There is a sound effect that does not match anything. There is a
zoom on the owner's face for no reason. The owner watches, and we hold on his
smile slowly, slowly dying — while his sister, beaming, says "he's so talented."

**Tag:** *"Love your nephew. Maybe don't employ your nephew."*

## 02 — THE BEDSHEET
**~30s · engine: slow reveal**

Open on the most gorgeous food cinematography imaginable. Butter melting in
slow motion. Steam curling through a shaft of golden light. A crumb falling at
120 frames per second. Swelling orchestral score. This is a Michelin
advertisement.

Pull back. Pull back. Pull back.

It's a guy in his garage. It's a muffin. It's on a wrinkled bedsheet. The
golden light is a desk lamp with a paper towel taped over it. He is holding his
phone with two hands and his tongue is sticking out in concentration.

He looks at the photo. He nods, deeply satisfied. "That's the one."

**Tag:** *"Your food is better than your photos."*

## 03 — SINCE 1994
**~35s · engine: escalation**

A beautiful hand-painted sign: *Family Owned Since 1994.* Warm, proud, earned.
The owner pats it.

"And the website's been up since day one."

Cut to the website. It is *also* from 1994. Visitor counter. Under-construction
GIF with the little animated shovel. Three different fonts, none of them good.
A background tile that repeats.

The owner, completely sincere: "It's timeless."

His daughter, off-camera: "Dad."

"It's *timeless.*"

**Tag:** *"Some things should last thirty years. Not that."*

---

## 04 — THE HOLD MUSIC
**~30s · engine: confident wrongness**

A customer on the phone, on hold. The hold music is... a man humming. Badly.
Close to the receiver. Occasionally he loses the tune and starts over. At one
point he clears his throat and keeps going.

Cut to the owner, arms folded, immensely proud: "I recorded it myself. Saved
four hundred dollars."

Cut back to the customer, who has now been listening to this for a while and
has developed a complicated facial expression.

Cut to the owner: "That's my own voice."

**Tag:** *"You saved four hundred dollars. It cost you the customer."*

## 05 — THE PHOTO
**~35s · engine: escalation · very relatable**

Every small business photo ever taken. The owner, arms crossed, standing stiffly
dead-center in front of their storefront, squinting directly into the sun.

His wife, holding the phone: "Smile more."

He smiles more. It is worse.

"Smile *normal.*"

He does not know what normal is anymore. Rapid montage of increasingly dead
expressions — arms crossed, arms uncrossed, one hand on hip, hands in pockets,
pointing at his own sign for some reason, doing a thumbs up he immediately
regrets.

Final frame: the actual photo, now printed enormous on the side of a van.

**Tag:** *"Twenty-two years of work. One photo. Let's fix the photo."*

## 06 — STORYTIME
**~45s · engine: deadpan contrast · true UGC format, 9:16 handheld**

Vertical. Handheld. A woman in her car, phone propped on the wheel, full
storytime energy.

"Okay so I need to tell y'all what happened with my bakery's page because I am
still not okay."

Enormous build. She is genuinely emotional. She gestures. She restarts a
sentence twice. She says "and *this* is the part that got me." She takes a
steadying breath. The stakes feel life-altering.

The reveal, delivered with total devastation: **"Someone commented 'looks dry.'"**

Beat. She stares into the middle distance.

"It's a *croissant*, Deborah. They're *supposed* to be flaky."

**Tag:** *"We can't stop Deborah. We can make the croissant look incredible."*

## 07 — THE SCROLL
**~30s · engine: slow reveal**

An owner on his couch at night, scrolling. He stops on a competitor's page.
It's gorgeous — slow motion, great light, a video that actually moves. He
watches it. Then watches it again.

He taps over to his own page.

One photo. Slightly blurry. It's of a parking lot. The caption reads
**"we're open."** Posted eight months ago.

Slow push in on his face. He is not sad. He is *deciding something.*

**Tag:** *"Your work is better than theirs. Your page isn't."*

---

## 08 — THE STRATEGY
**~30s · engine: confident wrongness · talking head**

A man in a hi-vis vest, arms folded, addressing the camera with the authority of
someone about to explain something complex.

"People ask me about my social media strategy."

Long pause. He nods slowly, gathering himself.

"I put up a photo of the truck."

Cut to the photo. It's a truck. It's fine.

Back to him: "Twenty nineteen."

Longer pause. Absolute conviction, no doubt in his body whatsoever:

"It did alright."

**Tag:** *"It did not do alright."*

---

## 09 — THE ELEVEN PM AD
**~35s · engine: escalation**

Kitchen table, 11pm, one lamp on. The owner is writing his own ad, hunting for
keys with two fingers. His wife is doing a crossword and not looking up.

He finishes. He is *thrilled.* He reads it aloud with real theatrical
commitment:

"*Quality service... at a price... that is also quality.*"

His wife does not look up. "Mm."

"Should I add exclamation points?"

"No."

He adds four exclamation points.

**Tag:** *"You're good at your job. This is not your job."*

---

## 10 — GRAND REOPENING
**~30s · engine: escalation**

A banner: **GRAND REOPENING — THIS WEEKEND ONLY!**

It is faded. It is sun-bleached to almost nothing. One corner has come loose and
is flapping.

Cut: same banner, seasons changing behind it. Snow. Then leaves. Then snow again.

The owner, on a ladder, straightening it with great care: "It still reads fine."

A customer walking past: "Wasn't this last year?"

Owner, not turning around: "It's ongoing."

**Tag:** *"Three years is not a weekend."*

## My pick of the ten

**06 STORYTIME** — the only true UGC format here, and the most shareable thing on
the list. Business owners will send it to each other.
**02 THE BEDSHEET** — the reveal is a genuinely great visual gag and it shows off
range: we shoot the beautiful version *and* the garage version.
**08 THE STRATEGY** — thirty seconds, one location, one performer, brutally
quotable. Cheapest to make, highest hit rate.

# How these get built

You said you don't make movies, so here's the whole thing plainly.

**A 40-second video is about 10 shots.** Most are 3–5 seconds on screen. You
almost never hold one image longer than that in comedy — the cut *is* the joke.

**Generate each shot as its own clip, then cut them together in Premiere.**
Seedance's minimum is 4 seconds, so generate at 4–5s and trim off what you don't
need. You'll use maybe 60% of each clip, and that's normal — that's what editing
is.

**Use a 15-second clip only when a performance has to hold** without a cut —
concept 08's monologue, concept 06's storytime build. Those need one continuous
take or the timing dies.

**The one discipline that matters: lock the character first.** The same owner has
to look like the same person in shot 1 and shot 9. So before generating any
video, generate *one* still of that character and feed it into every single shot
as a reference. Skip this and you get ten slightly different men and the video
is unusable. This is the single most common way these projects fail.

**Dialogue.** These are talking pieces, so plan on recording or generating the
voice separately and laying it under the picture in Premiere. Trying to get
native audio to nail a comic pause is a losing battle — comic timing is an
editing decision, and you want that dial in your hands.

**Rough shot budget per video:** 10–14 generated clips to end up with 10 keepers.
Unlimited means you should absolutely generate three versions of every joke beat
and pick the funniest.
