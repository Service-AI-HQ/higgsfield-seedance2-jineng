# PILOT — LOOKS DRY

**Target finished length:** 1:15–1:20 · **Format:** 9:16 · **Shots:** 21
**Generated material:** ~100s, trimmed to ~78s in the edit

The pilot. Everything gets learned here before any other story is generated.

---

## Why this one carries 75 seconds

Length comes from story, not from holding shots longer. This version adds two
things the 45-second draft didn't have:

1. **Stakes before the comment.** The shop is already struggling. Deborah's
   comment isn't the cause of anything — it's the last straw on a bad month.
   That's what makes a rude sentence about a croissant feel like it matters.
2. **A false fix.** Renée tries the obvious solution first — she takes better
   photos herself. It doesn't work. This is the beat that separates a real story
   from a bit, and it's the beat that buys the runtime honestly.

## Edit notes

**Shot 14 has no music and no joke.** It's the only place in the film where
nothing is trying to entertain you, which is exactly why the turn at 15 works.
The instinct in the edit will be to shorten it or score it. Don't.

**Shot 10 is the biggest laugh.** The "KINDA?!" needs air on both sides — a beat
before Miles answers, a beat after Renée reacts. Comic timing lives in the gap,
not the line.

**Music enters at shot 16 and not before.** Fifteen shots of room tone makes the
music land like a door opening. Score it from the top and you've spent the
effect before you need it.

**Shot 19's long beat is the whole ending.** Hold it past comfortable. If it
feels slightly too long in the edit, it's right.

## Production order

1. **Character sheets** — Renée, Miles, Deborah. Split-screen, photoreal
   unretouched preset, anti-airbrush module on. These are the foundation; if a
   face drifts, everything built on it is scrap.
2. **Location plates** — kitchen, shop front, car.
3. **Start frames** — one still per shot, 21 total, each referencing the locked
   character + location.
4. **Dialogue audio** — shots 6, 10, 11, 20. Generate before the video so it can
   go in as `audio_reference` and the mouths match.
5. **Video** — image-to-video from each start frame. Generate 2–3 variants of
   shots 10, 15, and 19; those three carry the film and are worth the extra
   passes.
6. **Assemble** — Premiere, or the Adobe tools available in this session.

## What the pilot is testing

- Does character identity hold across 21 shots from one sheet?
- Does dialogue passed as `audio_reference` actually sync?
- Does `use_unlim: true` work from this session, or is everything hand-run on web?
- How many variants does a comedy beat actually need before one lands?

Answers to those four determine how the other five stories get produced.
